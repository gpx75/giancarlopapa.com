This summer I walked to Santiago de Compostela. I started at home in Switzerland on St James’ Day, then took the Camino del Norte from Irun along the coast, and halfway through switched to the Camino Primitivo, the oldest of the routes, which crosses the mountains of Asturias and Galicia. From Santiago the journey continued through Portugal to Fátima.

Every stage was recorded on a Garmin Forerunner 965 and synced to Strava. These are the numbers, and some of the photos.

## Stage 0: Münchwilen → Winterthur

The Camino starts at your own front door. Mine started on 25 July, the feast of St James: I parked the car in Münchwilen and walked 26.5 km back home to Winterthur.

The next morning, at St. Marien in Oberwinterthur, I had my shoes, my feet and my scallop shell blessed. That evening I took the train from Winterthur to Zurich airport and flew to Amsterdam, where a booking mistake left me an unplanned night before the flight to Biarritz the next day. From there it is a short hop across the border to Irun, where the Camino del Norte begins.

### A radio interview in Pasaia

On the first morning, in Pasai Donibane, the village's parish priest was out on his morning walk — hiking clothes, poles, no habit — and walked part of the way with us, showing us the village. On the waterfront a reporter from a local radio station stopped him for an interview, and he talked about the Camino del Norte: "a bit more of a leg-breaker" than the Francés, but more varied, more rural, and a very rewarding experience.

### The evening talk in Güemes

At the albergue in Güemes, after a dinner paid for by the donations of the pilgrims who had stayed the night before, its 89-year-old founder stood up to speak. The room we were eating in, he said, was once his family's cow stable — and it is not a dining room but a museum, built from a journey through Africa and Latin America in the Land Rover parked outside. We arrive tired and hungry, he said, so we see only tables and food: we care a lot for the body and little for the spirit. Then he invited everyone to the small hermitage behind the albergue, where the paintings show the way of life, from manipulation to liberation — for him, the most important place on the whole Camino del Norte.

## Camino Primitivo: Oviedo → Santiago

From San Vicente de la Barquera I moved to Oviedo and started the Primitivo at the cathedral. This is the route King Alfonso II is said to have walked in the 9th century: inland, over the Cordillera Cantábrica into Galicia, joining the Camino Francés at Melide for the final days.

Between stages 21 and 22 I took the bus for the last stretch from Castroverde into Lugo, about 20 km, and started again from the city.

Stage 18, from Tineo to Berducedo, was the toughest day of the whole walk: the longest (42.5 km), the most climbing (almost 1,500 m) and the highest point (1,227 m).

The pilgrimage did not end at the cathedral, but the walking did: this part was not on foot, and none of it is in the numbers above. From Santiago I travelled south through Portugal — Porto, then Nazaré, where I went back to running on the beach, then Fátima, and finally Lisbon.

At Fátima, in the Chapel of the Apparitions, I offered the shell — the one blessed in Oberwinterthur and carried all the way to Santiago — and the whole Camino to Our Lady of Fátima. For me, that was where the journey closed.

## Afterwards

Twenty-five days, 677 km, and a lot of time to think with nothing to fix and nothing to deploy. I run, eat, pray and sleep, then repeat — the Camino was the same loop, just longer.

Every stage was recorded on Strava; my latest activities are on [/runs](/runs).
